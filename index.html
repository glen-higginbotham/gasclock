Economic and Policy Context

Australia is one of the world's largest exporters of liquefied natural gas (LNG). Despite shipping tens of billions of dollars worth of gas overseas annually, the Commonwealth consistently collects more tax revenue from the beer excise than from the Petroleum Resource Rent Tax (PRRT).

Generous carry-forward deductions, investment uplift rates, and structural transfer pricing rules allow multinational energy operators to export non-renewable Australian resources while reporting minimal taxable super-profits.

Macroeconomic Balance

When governments rely predominantly on income tax rather than resource levies, the burden of cooling inflation falls almost exclusively on Reserve Bank interest rate rises, directly squeezing mortgage holders and renters.

International Contrast

Norway taxes offshore petroleum profits at an effective rate of 78%, which has funded a national sovereign wealth fund valued at over US$1.7 trillion. Australia has largely liquidated equivalent volumes of gas with minimal long-term public capital retained.
